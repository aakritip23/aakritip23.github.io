About me

I'm Aakriti, a final-year student at IIT Kanpur. On paper I study Mechanical Engineering. In practice, most of my time here has gone into one question: why do people use a product, why do they stop, and what does the data say about both?

That question has pulled me in two directions. As a product intern at Stash AI, I helped turn an idea, an AI doubt-solver for JEE students in smaller Indian cities, into an app that more than ten thousand students used. I spent weeks reading survey responses, talking to students and watching where they dropped off. On the other side, I like building things myself. I've built a RAG assistant that reads research papers so chemists don't have to, a causal engine that explains why a business metric moved, and a team of AI agents that runs outbound sales.

I'm happiest between the two: when a product question needs someone who can also open the notebook and check the answer. Outside of that, I've spent a year running start-up programmes at E-Cell, organised TEDxIITKanpur, mentored students at Prayas, and I'm a trained Bharatanatyam dancer.

Experience

  1. AI/ML Engineer Intern · Medetronix Labs

    May – Aug 2026

    Picking parameters for an electrochemistry experiment usually means digging through a pile of papers. I built a tool that does that reading. It pulls papers from OpenAlex, finds the passages that matter, and asks an LLM to recommend parameters for seven techniques. The hard part was retrieval, so I added a second filtering stage that only passes the most relevant context to the model. That made the answers more accurate and faster. The finished app (FastAPI and Svelte) matched expert values about 77% of the time for cyclic voltammetry, and I left with a letter of recommendation.

  2. ML Research Intern · Ashok Leyland

    Dec 2025 – Jan 2026

    Fatigue simulations for vehicle parts need vibration levels as inputs, and getting them was slow. I first automated the conversion of raw vibration recordings into frequency spectra, which cut that step down to almost nothing. Then I narrowed 31 input parameters to the 5 that mattered and trained an XGBoost model to predict average and peak vibration, reaching an R² of about 0.85.

  3. Product Management Intern · Stash AI

    May – Aug 2025

    I joined when Stash AI's doubt-solving app for JEE students was still an idea. The first weeks were all listening: over a thousand survey responses, around twenty student interviews and a close look at five competitors. That shaped the MVP, which offered round-the-clock doubt solving with light gamification to bring students back. I wrote the PRD, wireframed about 30 screens and ran sprints with design and engineering. After launch, we used Mixpanel and A/B tests to fix the screens where students were dropping off, and bounce rate fell by about a fifth.
